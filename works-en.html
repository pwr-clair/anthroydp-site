01

Che Go Eun

Do I Dream of Electric Sheep?
2026 · Digital print on self-adhesive film and fabric, dimensions variable

In this work, Goeun Che presents the process of training a generative image AI on her own painterly style as an artistic experiment. Rather than defining the artist and AI as the respective creator and tool, the work explores how their roles are continuously renegotiated through the processes of image generation and circulation. The images produced by the AI unsettle the boundaries between original and copy, creation and learning, prompting renewed questions about authorship and the meaning of artistic practice. Furthermore, the artist draws attention to the paradoxical nature of AI: while it is readily projected with both feminine and masculine qualities, it possesses no gender of its own. In doing so, the work reveals the structures of identity and desire that humans continue to inscribe onto technology.

►Che Go Eun

After training in Korean painting, Che Go relocated to Europe, where she has spent the past decade working primarily with digital imagery. By combining traditional painting with computational technologies including digital collage, 3D modeling, robotic drawing, virtual reality, and AI-generated images, she investigates the ontological connections and ruptures that emerge between the painter and image-generating technologies, while exploring the expanded possibilities of painting as a medium. Drawing inspiration from female narratives found in love, video games, beauty culture, folklore, and religion, she develops works that generate new visual imaginaries from these diverse sources.

02

Chang Younghae

Grass, Oxygen, Light, Friends
2026 · Video installation

Chang Younghae’s work focuses on the physical conditions required to compress and render the image of a vast grassland within the confined space of indoor screen golf. This extreme compression of space involves high-speed imaging technologies that capture split-second moments, simulation systems, and even the dust produced as walls withstand tens of thousands of impacts. In this sense, the seamless image of a virtual 3D grassland bears traces of physical impact, friction, and wear beneath its surface. This work extends from the single-channel video cobalt (2025), which focuses on a mountain where a golf course was built atop the site of a civilian massacre that, to this day, has yet to be fully excavated or preserved. The artist’s earlier exploration of “the ways in which layers of land overlap” now extends into the virtual space of screen golf. Chang films a screen-golf environment in which the actual course in Gyeongsan, North Gyeongsang Province, featured in cobalt has been reproduced in 3D, tracing the technological conditions through which physical strikes and impacts are transformed, via a virtual landscape, into entertainment. Layered onto this exploration is the particular class dimension that golf carries in South Korean society, revealing a complex terrain in which technology and nature, leisure and violence, the real and the virtual become intertwined.

►Chang Younghae

Younghae Chang works across video, performance, and installation, focusing on the structures through which violence and desire transform and distort the body. For the artist, the body exists as an entity that is reshaped and reconfigured through technology. Employing mediating apparatuses such as cameras, AI, and X-ray imaging, Chang examines processes that convert the body into images, fragment it, and render it objectified.

03

Sla Joo

displace - - -
2026 · Mixed media, variable installation

Research is a process of reading a place, but can a single place ever be read in only one way? This question resembles the structure of mandam—a conversational form in which meanings are continually produced, only to slip away again. The work extends this process through the concept of displace. Experiences gathered by walking through the site and images of the Gyeongin railway collected from the internet point to the same place, yet generate different modes of reading. Through displacement, the images on the screen shift from visual information into height data, and through the layer-by-layer process of 3D printing, they become material once again. In this process, the railway is no longer an object of representation; instead, it emerges as an ongoing movement in which ways of reading a place are translated into different positions and material states. A single place repeatedly circulates between photographs, data, calculated surfaces, and layered matter, continually being read anew, with each reading giving rise to another act of tracing.

►Sla Joo

Sla Joo's practice centers on themes of fragmentation and transformation, informed by influences from animation and literature. I am drawn to narratives in which bodies are altered, identities are dismantled, and new forms of subjectivity emerge. Such narratives offer a way to reflect on how identity is continuously fractured and reconfigured within contemporary society. Through the use of multiple media, I explore transitions between different material and representational forms, often introducing intentional glitches and errors into the process. These disruptions become a means of visualizing the anxiety, instability, and transformation that accompany the breakdown and reconstruction of identity, while opening up possibilities for new narrative structures.

04

Youngkak Cho

Things No One Could Keep (無主之物)
2026 · Perforated-steel scores, robotic light-sensor performance system, Morse-code-based sound on DAWless hardware, field recordings, variable installation

Things No One Could Keep (無主之物) is a media installation that composes the district data of Cheonggyecheon, Mullae, and Seongsu, areas from which Seoul’s manufacturing ecosystem has drained away, into three pieces of music, and consists of three light scores, steel plates perforated with the Morse code of the sentence each piece conceals; a robot that deciphers the scores with light sensors and performs them on DAWless hardware; and field recordings collected in the three districts. The noise of the city becomes the background of what remains, the Morse signal becomes the rhythm of what has vanished, and the sentences, inscribed nowhere as text, exist only in the intervals of light.

►Youngkak Cho

Youngkak Cho works across media art, artificial intelligence, and design. For more than a decade he has explored ways of treating AI not as a tool but as a condition of the work, recently shifting from generation-centered practice toward the infrastructural conditions of AI as subject matter.

05

Kayoung Choi

The Beef Noodle Twist · My Beef Noodle Diary
The Beef Noodle Twist 2026 · Two-channel video, color, sound, 16 min. 12 sec.
My Beef Noodle Diary 2026 · Interactive game (web-based research map), HTML/JavaScript, color, dimensions variable

My Beef Noodle Diary and The Beef Noodle Twist are a game and video work that traces Kayoung Choi’s research journey across Korea, Japan, and Taiwan, using canned food as a cartographic device. For the artist, the can functions as both a rolled-up map and a mobile archive—a symbolic medium that brings distant places and temporalities into contact. Originally developed to transport food across greater distances, canned beef gave rise to Naju gomtang in Korea and beef noodle soup in Taiwan, connecting distinct culinary traditions and historical trajectories. These routes of circulation further extend into a geographical network that links Kaohsiung with Incheon and Shimonoseki, and Naju with New Taipei, Yunlin, and Kyoto. Belonging to a generation that did not directly experience colonialism or the Cold War, the artist follows these itineraries to look simultaneously toward the histories of a century ago and the disasters of the present. Ultimately, the gaze that once traveled to distant places folds back upon itself, returning to the present and inviting us to look anew at the lives we inhabit here and now.

►Kayoung Choi

Kayoung Choi takes tangible objects present in the here and now as clues, tracing memories and imaginings of times and places she has never experienced. She collects these objects, travels to the sites where they originally belonged, and moves in pursuit of the histories and personal memories bound up in them. Positioning herself at a distant coordinate and then looking back toward this side, her fieldwork folds and rolls up the map of times and spaces that could never be joined, drawing a new trajectory across it. Setting up a proportional equation between others and the self, past and present, she gives form to questions addressed to who we are now through painting, video, installation, and performance.

06

Siheun Kim

Breathing Residues: Rules of a Synthetic Ecology
2026 · Mixed-media apparatus combining pneumatic kinetic installation and video (Dispositif), variable installation

Breathing Residues: Rules of a Synthetic Ecology is a video-and-sculpture dispositif that translates the digital temporality of a synthetic ecosystem generated within a game engine into the physical rhythm of breathing through a pneumatic apparatus. The video (Part A) presents a world inhabited by proliferating microorganisms, human–machine hybrids shaped by industrial rhythms, and a technological system that seeks to observe, classify, and control them. The hybrids embody the fatigue of labor and the slow corrosion of metal, while the microorganisms inhabit the damp temporality of proliferation and accumulation. Entangled within the synthetic ecosystem, these forms of alterity leave temporal traces upon one another’s surfaces, resisting complete measurement and control. Linked to the video, the pneumatic sculpture (Part B) responds to the viewer’s presence through sensors. Delayed and imperfect signals are translated into subtle changes in the moving image and the sculpture’s slow cycles of expansion and contraction. Rather than synchronizing the digital and the physical, the work foregrounds the residual gap between them, inviting viewers to inhabit a contact zone where translation remains incomplete and meaning emerges through delay, misalignment, and residue.

►Siheun Kim

Siheun Kim explores the contemporary condition in which technology senses and translates the world, drawing on the sculptural languages of the othered body and fluid mediation. Through site-based research, she collects ecological and historical traces and reconfigures them through game-engine-based moving images, 3D printing, sensors, and kinetic systems. Through the Dispositif—a hybrid moving-image and sculptural apparatus that brings these elements together—Kim gives sensory form to the ways bodies, places, and nonhuman temporalities remain irreducible to any single image or informational form.

Heesoo Kwon

The Orange Room
2026 · Lenticular light box, photographs in vintage frames, curtain and hanger from the artist’s house, typewritten pages in frame, variable installation

The Orange Room began with a hypnosis session held in Kwon’s first studio after relocating to Los Angeles in 2026. During the session, she performed a ritual blessing the room in orange light—a moment that revealed room, womb, and archive as a single, continuous structure. A lenticular light box, photographs in vintage frames, a curtain and hanger from the artist’s home, and typewritten pages connect images from different periods of her life through the recurring signal of orange light, forming the first page of an ongoing living archive of personal memory.

►Heesoo Kwon

Heesoo Kwon works on Leymusoom, a ritualistic and auto-ethnographic archive project rooted in her own and her family's life, matrilineal genealogies, and Korean mythology. Using digital technology as a tool of creation, she traces ancestral memories to reimagine patriarchal narratives. Begun in 2017, the project is an archive of 3D animation and photography combining analog and digital media—a shrine to Kwon's invented "autobiographical feminist religion," envisioning a utopia where her female ancestors coexist.

08

Jongwan Jang

Omega Point · A Gift from Kilimanjaro
Omega Point 2023 · A Gift from Kilimanjaro 2024
Acrylic gouache on linen, pigment, each 227.3 × 181.8 cm

Omega Point and A Gift from Kilimanjaro are part of a series of large-scale paintings first presented in Jang Jongwan’s 2023 solo exhibition Goldilocks Zone. Centered on agriculture and plant life, the works unfold the artist’s distinctive surreal landscapes. Vast geometrically cultivated farmlands, genetically modified plants, and domesticated livestock—forms of nature reshaped to serve human needs—evoke a distinctly science-fictional sensibility that has become a defining motif in his practice. By recomposing these familiar yet estranged landscapes, Jang envisions possible sites of future settlement. Continuing this line of inquiry, the works presented in this exhibition appropriate the iconography of prophets and political leaders found in religious paintings and propaganda imagery, visualizing humanity’s enduring desire to approach—or even occupy—the realm of the divine through technological advancement.

►Jongwan Jang

Jongwan Jang portrays the endless anxiety of human-centered societies and modern humanity, which emphasizes selfish rationality, from a peculiarly warm but cynical perspective. The artist's works combine colorful and seemingly lighthearted images with his acute view of contemporary society. The ironic landscape maximizes its kitschiness through the use of animal hide, symbolizing the human desire for salvation, the neglect of nature and animals, and foolish, blind faith.

Haejung Jung, Psients (Mikael Hwang)

Bio Odyssey: In Search of Phantom Yeast · Inflating
Bio Odyssey: In Search of Phantom Yeast 2026 · Digital animation, Full HD, color, 3 min. 10 sec.
Inflating 2026 · Aluminum foil tape on yeast-inflated balloons, dimensions variable
Co-created by Haejung Jung, Psients (Mikael Hwang), and the 15 participants of the workshop “Bio Odyssey: In Search of Phantom Yeast”

Co-organized by the Yeongdeungpo Cultural Foundation and the Chong Kun Dang Kochon Foundation, Bio Odyssey: In Search of Phantom Yeast is a collaborative workshop project bringing together 15 participants (grades 5–7) with the artists Psients (Mikael Hwang) and Haejung Jung. Together they collected and observed airborne yeast in Yeongdeungpo, then gave it visible form through kinetic sculptures, character design, and animation.

►Haejung Jung, Psients (Mikael Hwang)

Haejung Jung and Psients collaborated to conceive and lead a bio-workshop and co-creation project with fifteen children and adolescents from the Yeongdeungpo district. Haejung Jung works primarily in 3D animation, video, and installation, exploring the entanglements of human and nonhuman entities across the boundaries of nature, culture, technology, and matter. Psients (Mikael Hwang) is a scientist-turned-artist and the Director of Critical Biology, an artistic research laboratory based in Seoul, whose practice investigates the intersections of biology, sound, and music.

Jaeha Ban

Where What Passes Overhead Arrives
2026 · Real-time data-driven multi-channel installation, mixed media, dimensions variable

Many of the numbers and information about North Korea are not measured directly on the ground, but derived from traces captured by satellites. For this work, Ban Jaeha uses publicly available satellite data to observe thirteen cities in North Korea and reconstruct economic indicators by following methodologies used by South Korean research institutions. At the same time, he assigns non-economic names to the same observations, revealing how limited observations are interpreted and named to become numbers and knowledge about North Korea. In doing so, the work questions how numbers and knowledge that appear to be objective facts are themselves constructed through processes of observation, interpretation, and naming, prompting us to reflect on how our own knowledge and judgments about North Korea are formed.

►Jaeha Ban

Jaeha Ban takes moments in which systems malfunction as the primary material of her practice. Her work is grounded in research that observes and traces the marks of circulation, technology, and division embedded in everyday objects and events. Through performance, installation, and video, she juxtaposes the synchronous flows produced by systems of circulation and technological infrastructure with the asynchronous conditions generated by the division of the Korean Peninsula. Ban seeks to transform points of systemic malfunction into sites where new forms of perception are activated.

Gemini Kim

Technoeotbak & Street Scribbles
2026 · Single channel video, ILDA laser projection, phosphorescent wall, AR application, mixed media, dimensions variable
Collaborating artists: Dahye Jeong (Graphic, AR, Jimi Churros), Hyeonseo Lee (Graphic, Laser)

Technoeotbak & Street Scribbles explores the subtle traces embedded in the urban landscape and the lives they contain through contemporary technological media. Beginning with Jimi Churros, a project tracing the graffiti tag Kim Ji-mi Click discovered in northern Seoul, the work reconstructs the streetscape and subcultural traces of Yeongdeungpo through video, laser projection, and augmented reality (AR). Drawing on Guy Debord’s concept of the dérive and the philosophical notion of the middle voice, the work positions the artist between observing the city and being carried along by its rhythms. Graffiti and Technoeotbak—an off-beat dance culture distinctive to Yeongdeungpo—become guides through the city’s shifting landscape, where urban traces are continually reconfigured. Through AI-assisted image processing and laser drawing, graffiti and bodily gestures are transformed into new material forms. Moving laser projections, phosphorescent afterimages, and AR overlays bring the nocturnal landscape of Yeongdeungpo into the gallery, where ephemeral urban traces are temporarily preserved. Encountering these intersections of subculture and technology, visitors are invited to reflect on the diverse rhythms through which ordinary lives unfold within larger technological systems.

►Gemini Kim

Gemini Kim is an artist originally from Incheon. Although he carries a bit of a complex about how Korea focuses so heavily on central Seoul, he ironically desires to live in the heart of Seoul more than anyone else. Focusing on the concept of 'the periphery,' his projects track regional changes over time. He also founded the 'Korean Association of Subalterns' to study this peripheral mentality, aiming to bring hidden stories from outside the mainstream cultural world into the spotlight.

Dew Kim

Kissing Forever ∞ Kiss of Chaos ☆
2020 · Single channel video, 56 sec. (loop)

Borrowing the form of a K-pop music video, Kiss of Chaos ☆ examines the blurring boundaries of queerness and gender through the lens of shamanism. Against the binaries inherited from Western religion - good and evil, subject and object - the work turns to the ritual practice of North Asian shamanism, long dismissed by Europeans as “black faith,” to unsettle those divisions. Performing as the idol persona HornyHoneydew, the artist stages “becoming” as a way of surfacing the desire of one who falls outside normativity, moving toward a chaotic world that endlessly splits and expands.

►Dew Kim

Dew Kim explores the body at the point where religion, queerness, and posthuman identity collide. He treats the body not as a fixed form but as a site where faith, resistance, and transformation meet, asking how it comes to carry vulnerability, desire, and histories it did not choose. Moving between sculpture, installation, and performance, he examines how power operates within gestures of devotion, discipline, and longing. Through performances of "becoming" - becoming a shaman, becoming an idol - he weaves the aesthetics of popular culture together with ritual symbolism, creating theatrical spaces where pleasure and vulnerability meet.

13

Sun Choi

Quiet Answer
2026 · Single channel video, 2 min. 4 sec.

This work originated from a brief encounter fourteen years ago between Sun Choi and a young Zainichi Korean in Yokohama, Japan. Unable to speak Korean, the young man was asked to repeatedly say his own name while the artist recorded the exchange as an experimental video. Through this simple act of repetition, the work explores the name not merely as a linguistic marker of identity, but as a performative site where history, memory, emotion, and subjectivity converge. As the exchange unfolds, the young man’s speech gradually slows and softens, revealing subtle emotional shifts that move between laughter and tears. The video exposes the gap between one’s name and one’s sense of self, inviting reflection on the layered nature of identity beyond the limits of language.

►Sun Choi

Sun Choi transforms the material traces of events unfolding within the society he inhabits into the substance of his artistic practice. Employing materials marked by social incidents including saliva left on the street, grease seeping from the carcasses of pigs buried after mass culling, foam collected from wastewater sediment, and capsaicin spray used by police for crowd control, he creates what he calls "unpainted paintings," minimizing deliberate brushwork wherever possible. Through the transformation of these materials, his practice converts social reality into artistic meaning. Extending beyond painting, his work encompasses video, sound, and spatial installations, expanding across diverse media while remaining grounded in the materiality of contemporary social experience.
